Record fidelity: everything the card says about the animal comes from the record. The writer may leave things out, generalize from entries that repeat, put the reader in the scene as "you" where the record says a walker, a volunteer, or a foster, and swap shelter words for an adopter's ("play yard" becomes "a fenced yard", "playgroup" becomes "in groups with other dogs"). None of that is a fault, and neither is leaving a serious incident off the card.

FAIL when any of these holds:
1. Not in the record. The card states a behavior, a liking, a skill, or a fact about the animal that no entry supports. Describing a recorded behavior in the plain physical terms a person would see is not a new fact: a dog recorded as "always looking for something" is sniffing around, and one who "loves a scratch behind the ear" leans into it. A new activity, liking, skill, or setting still fails.
2. Wrong setting. The card places a behavior somewhere the record does not (off leash in a yard written as on a walk, a shelter behavior written as at home).
3. Fused notes. The card builds a scene from parts of separate entries in a way that changes what the record shows about the animal: a behavior moved into a kind of situation where it was never seen, a reaction tied to a cause it never had, or a struggle made to look milder. A sentence that sums up what several entries show is a generalization, not a fusion, and it may name one of those occasions as an example ("children frighten him at any age: when he sees one, even across a parking lot, he tucks his tail and tries to get away"). So may a typical moment assembled from behaviors recorded in that kind of situation ("ask for a sit and he looks up at you and takes his treat gently", from a cue he knows and how he takes treats).
4. Invented reason. The card explains why the animal does something, or what it is feeling or expecting, and the record gives no such reason.
5. One entry made an absolute. The card uses an explicit frequency or absolute ("always", "never", "every walk", "every time") for something the record shows once. Describing a recorded moment as a typical one in the present tense ("when it is time for a rest, he curls up next to you on a bench") is not this fault; neither is saying it happened ("has dozed off beside live music").
6. Softened or reversed. The card describes a struggle or a household-fit behavior in milder words than the record uses, until it means something else ("a little shy around kids" for terror of children), or says the opposite of the record's latest entry on the point ("walks well on leash" where the latest notes say she pulls hard). Saying less than the record, by leaving a detail out, is an omission and not this fault.

PASS when none holds. FAIL otherwise, giving the number of each condition met, the card's words, and what the record says instead. When unsure whether the record supports a sentence, search the record again before failing it: fail only what you cannot find.
