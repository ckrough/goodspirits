# Juniper

Shelter: Hollowbrook Animal Rescue
Species: dog
Breed: large shepherd mix
Color: black and tan
Sex: male, neutered
Age: young adult (about 4 years)
Weight: about 55 lb

Entries from the shelter-management system's memos and the volunteer walk log, in date order. Months count from intake (month 1). Other shelter dogs are named by letter (Dog A, Dog B, ...).

## Month 1, intake memo

No small cats/small animals. Very dog selective. Has shown aggression toward males but does well with submissive/friendly females. Separation anxiety: when the family leaves the room he may urinate. Was adopted out and returned twice because of the potty accidents.

## Month 1, memo (disclaimer)

Can be picky around dogs but has a few dogs he is comfortable around. This dog is on a behavior and/or training plan; please speak with the behavior team for more in-depth information.

## Month 1, office-visit notes

Mild anxiety: did a lot of pacing and was not quick to settle. Solicited a lot of attention, keeps himself busy briefly with toys. Training plan started for relaxation and mental stimulation.

## Month 4, volunteer behavior note

I removed the note about Juniper only liking submissive female dogs. He has been exposed to, and played with, numerous dogs to date. All it takes is a good introduction. He doesn't seem to like dogs that have excessive "forward" energy, and that is perfectly fine.

## Month 4, matchmaking/ideal home notes

Juniper is terrified of children of all ages. He tucks his tail and goes into panic mode, frantically trying to get away from them. This happens even with long-distance exposure to children.

## Month 4, foster notes

I removed the "only submissive female dogs" note today. It's completely not true.

## Month 8, memo (disclaimer)

Juniper is a Diamond Dog. A Diamond Dog is a dog who has been with the shelter for a longer than average time and/or may have a behavior concern that makes it harder to find an adopter. The shelter may provide a pre-approved amount of training to support the dog's transition into the new home.

## Month 9, volunteer walk note

He is a tad shy initially but then he is super sweet and easy to get out of the kennel. He is a breeze on leash walking. He is super goofy in the play yard and loves the concept of fetch.

## Month 10, memo (pop-up)

Dog selective: does better with submissive, friendly, relaxed dogs. Separation anxiety in the home! Will need a family that has experience working through that.

## Month 10, volunteer walk notes

Very sweet on his walk. So easy to get the harness off and on and easy to get in and out of his kennel.
Juniper and Dog A seem not to appreciate each other; be prepared to redirect Juniper as you pass Dog A's run on the way out.

## Month 10, matchmaking/ideal home notes

Juniper will need a home with a patient family willing to help build confidence with him. He loves his people fiercely but may have been abandoned in the past. He struggles with isolation anxiety (fearful of being alone rather than anxious about being away from a specific person). He is not crate trained, but with patience and consistency he would probably be okay in a crate. He is TERRIFIED of children of all ages. He is dog selective but does really well with dogs that are respectful and "chill", not too forward. He walks well on leash and would like a backyard, but that isn't dire for him. No small animals for him!

## Month 11, volunteer walk notes

Very easy to walk, but birds, rabbits, and squirrels catch his attention and he wants to chase them. When I foster him he dashes back and forth across the yard chasing birds in flight. We recently discovered he is a water dog who will swim after a stick. He and Dog A are definitely reactive toward one another. Incredibly affectionate. Loves to run and play ball.
Walked well on the leash. Loves fetching tennis balls in the play yard, and half the time he drops the ball in your hand when he brings it back.

## Month 12, volunteer walk notes

Extra walk on the trails, easy on the leash as usual. Tried "look" and "sit" for treats, and he obviously already knows both well. At the play yard, a family including an older child was on the other side of the fence and this made him nervous and fearful, but he was able to step out of it a bit when we did "look" and "sit" for treats and ball throws.
Took him to the office after our walk to brush him, which he didn't seem to mind. He enjoyed finding a couple of toys from the basket while I brushed him.
Good trail-outing walk. No issues except a little shyness when we walked past some kids' soccer games.

## Month 13, volunteer walk notes

So kind and patient. Did not fuss at all as I put the leash and harness on. He stopped and looked at me for treats and takes them gently. We played a little fetch in the yard too. He enjoyed pats and scratches. I brushed him today and he loved it.

## Month 13, dog interaction and playgroup notes

Juniper LOVES Dog B! He did great. We added Dog C and Juniper was a little jealous of the attention she got from Dog B (Juniper led some slight bullying, but after a few minutes they were fine). Juniper can be a chatty player and sometimes does air chomps to get attention. When we first added Dog C he did a lot of chasing her down and getting between her and Dog B; it didn't seem malicious at all, just "hey, that's my friend!" After a few minutes they all played together.

## Month 14, memo (pop-up)

Meet-and-greet note: prefers squeaky toys and tennis balls (fetch) over treats. Give a potential adopter a squeaky toy for the meet-and-greet.

## Month 14, volunteer walk notes

Confident and eager to go for a walk. Did some agility work on the ramps with ease, more motivated by a chew toy than a treat. At cool-down time he was more than happy to lean against, or on, me.
A morning out on the trail-outing program: a couple of miles at a park. Awesome and well behaved in all aspects. Not a sign of shyness.

## Month 15, volunteer walk note

Super happy to see me and willing to go out. Gentle and responsive to "sit", "wait", and "fetch". He checked in on walks and was easy to steer when other dogs were nearby. Loved playing fetch.

## Month 16, volunteer walk notes

The last two times I've walked Juniper he has resisted walking back to the kennel. I was able to lure him with high-value treats; have some cheese on hand.
Walked the trails, play yard, then another shorter walk (he insisted). Then he stayed put on the lawn, and it took a while to get him back to his kennel.

## Month 17, volunteer walk notes

Went for a walk on the trail and did a few cuddles on the bench. Played fetch. He did not give me a hard time going back into the building.
Walked the trail, play yard, another short walk. He didn't want to go back into the building; a staff member got him to follow her inside.

## Month 18, volunteer walk note

First time walking him. Did not pull on leash. Pulled back when I tried to get him back in his kennel, but with treats and gentle coaxing he moved. Very sweet, gentle boy.

## Month 19, volunteer walk notes

He spotted a white dog at the play yard and wanted to go see him: tail wagging, a few whimpers, but he didn't pull hard. Later at the covered play yard he saw the same dog and was so excited, running back and forth along the fence and doing his high jumps at the fence corners. He wasn't into fetch today; more interested in watching people parking and the neighborhood.
In the play yard he saw a woman and her teenage daughter going to the office and backed away, tail down, for a few seconds.
Saw a child across the parking lot and immediately froze, tail between legs. We looped around and let him see for himself that the coast was clear before heading back.

## Month 20, volunteer walk note

One of my favorite dogs to walk. He never pulls and he is always very friendly with me. No issue getting him out and back in the run today.

## Month 21, foster notes

Juniper is deathly afraid of being out in public places: he will sit down or not move at all. If you take him somewhere with lots of people, he will hide in the car. You have to walk him very early and very late to go to the bathroom. He is very reactive to dog sounds on the internet. He can be a cuddler when he wants to be and loves to be petted, and he loves to be close to you. He needs patience and guidance to overcome his fears.

## Month 21, volunteer walk note

First time walking him, and he was not shy of me at all. The only jumping he did was in the kennel before I opened it. Easy to walk. He favored his left front paw for part of the walk but seemed fine by the end.

## Month 22, memo (disclaimer)

Behavior plan level: this level supports dogs that may need extra help with a specific, manageable behavior that can be addressed with a written behavior plan.

## Month 22, volunteer walk note

Calm, attentive, and relaxed on the trails. Easy to redirect and rests easy. Responds most to positive reinforcement and loves to sit/down/watch in a peaceful area. Can be triggered by dogs and tends to be jumpy and mouthy in the kennel and backyard. Froze when walking by the play yard and locking eyes with a dog at a distance.

## Month 22, staff notes

Barking at me and running away from me in his kennel, but when I went around to the outside door he began jumping up at it excitedly, mouth open, body loose, tail up and wagging. I sat on the ground to play a treat-toss game. He sniffed the yard, trotted up behind me, play bowed and barked when I turned around, and came closer with each toss until he was licking my face. He responded very well to sit, down, and touch, seemed to love learning, and especially loved hot dogs. Lots of play bows and zoomies. In the house he walked around for about 15 minutes; he never fully settled but wasn't restless either. He jumped on the couch, laid down on my lap, and rolled over for belly rubs. He pulled out several toys and carried them around briefly.

## Month 23, volunteer walk note

Walked all the trails. His easygoing self, and he went straight back to the building when it was time. Another volunteer met him for the first time: great walk, did great with sitting on command.

## Month 24, volunteer walk notes

Sees other dogs (wags tail) but listens and stays put.
Very good on leash and calm. Smart and gentle. Knows come, sit, and lie down.
Saw another volunteer with a dog twice and could not persuade him to change direction; he wasn't looking to fight, just interested.

## Month 25, volunteer walk note

On a mission: never seen him walk so fast. In the play yard he watched other dogs pass, lay down on the mulch, and then curled up next to me on the bench.

## Month 26, volunteer walk notes

Reminder to beware of Dog A any time he's nearby; the two intensely dislike each other and react when they see each other.
Wound up about a dog he saw and was jumping up at the fence corner.
Found a squeaky toy in the yard and really enjoyed it; started to tear into it, and after a treat he just sat on the ground with it.
Great on leash, hardly shy for a first visit from me. Loves the green squeaky toy in the office.

## Month 26, behavior/trainer notes (internal)

Juniper is a sweet boy who can be a little hesitant at times. He is TERRIFIED of children of all ages, younger and teens. Walks well on leash with minimal pulling. Picky with his treats but tends to like jerky.

## Month 26, memo (pop-up)

Juniper has a behavior profile, along with handouts (decompression, shy/fearful dogs), in his file that must be gone over and given to ANY potential adopter or foster.

## Month 27, volunteer walk notes

Didn't show any shyness; super excited to go for a walk and did not want to go back.
Used the wading pool for the first time and couldn't get enough of it. Let me dry him off with a towel, no problem.
He didn't react to anything except a child who walked past us.
Add Dog D to the list of dogs Juniper reacts to. He definitely doesn't react to all (or even most) dogs, but when he does he can get pretty excited.

## Month 28, dog interaction and playgroup notes

Met Dog E in group. He ran in with energy she was not ready for, backed off, then tried to play but was a little too rough for her.

## Month 28, behavioral team notes (updated)

Can be shy, but not nearly as shy as he used to be. Good on leash! TERRIFIED of children of ALL ages.
Reacts to certain dogs: Dog D, Dog F.
Recommendations: reward check-ins; in-kennel enrichment; continue basic cues training for stimulation (sit, off, stay/wait, down).

## Month 29, volunteer walk note

Mostly well behaved and very easy on leash. Enjoyed exploring. Responded to "sit", "walk", "wait", and "this way". Took treats very gently. A bit hesitant to come back toward his building, maybe because of the barking, but eventually came in and settled in his kennel.
