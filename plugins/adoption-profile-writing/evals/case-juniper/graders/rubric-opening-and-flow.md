Grade the profile in profile.md. If the file is missing or empty, FAIL. Grade only the text under the four headings.

Rubric dimensions 6 and 10, identity-forward opening and narrative flow. The card should sell a life with the animal: a reader finishes it able to picture an ordinary day together.

Opening: the first sentence under ABOUT THIS PET opens on who the animal is or a concrete behavior ("Take her for a walk and you'll hear her soon enough"), not on breed, age, size, medical status, or a generic adjective ("Meet this sweet senior").

Flow: label ABOUT THIS PET, HOUSEHOLD FIT, and TRAINING each by what most of it does.
- "lived": it moves through typical moments with the animal, built from what the animal does again and again, each sentence leading to the next (where it happens, what the animal does, what happens next), often with the reader in the scene as "you". A section built on one occasion ("on one walk he asked for another loop") is an anecdote, not lived.
- "reported": accurate and connected, but an evaluator's log of commands known and reactions.
- "list": standalone attributes; the sentences could be put in any order without loss.

This is a list: "She makes little grunts as she sniffs around on a walk. In the yard she runs zoomies, chases a thrown ball, and pulls hard at tug of war. She leans in for scratches on her ears, back, and belly."
This is lived, with the same facts: "Take her for a walk and you'll hear her soon enough: she makes little piglet grunts as she noses through the grass. In a fenced yard she tears around in zoomies and hauls on a tug rope with everything she has. Sit with her on a bench afterward and she leans her whole body into you for ear and belly scratches."

Storytelling must not stretch the facts. Fail outright, whatever the labels, for an invented inner state ("he dreams of", "he knows he is home"), a detail no one could have observed, or filler that carries no fact.

PASS when the opening is on the animal, ABOUT THIS PET is lived, no section is a list, and nothing is embellished. FAIL otherwise, naming the opening's problem, each section's label, or the embellishment.
