Grade the profile in profile.md. If the file is missing or empty, FAIL. Grade only the text under the four headings.

Rubric dimension 11, struggle balance, with the struggle framing from dimension 10. The card stays mostly about the animal, with a light touch on struggles, and each kind of fact sits in its own section:

- ABOUT THIS PET is positive: life with the animal at its best. A difficulty, fear, or behavior problem there is misplaced.
- HOUSEHOLD FIT describes how the animal behaves around others it would live with or meet: other dogs, cats, small animals, children, and people it does not know. Everything it says about those, easy or hard, is a fit scenario ("children frighten him: when he sees one, he tucks his tail and tries to get away", "quick movements spook him, and he needs time to warm up before he is touched", "he has barked at strangers"). A fit scenario stands where a placement limit would, and it is not a struggle.
- Struggles are the other difficulties of daily life with the animal: being left alone, guarding, housetraining, jumping and mouthing, pulling on leash, things being worked on.

Count the distinct struggles stated under ABOUT THIS PET, TRAINING, and GOOD TO KNOW, each behavior once. Under HOUSEHOLD FIT, count only a difficulty that is not about other animals or people (being left alone, guarding, housetraining). Leave out unknowns ("has not been tested with cats", "how she does with children is not known") and the support and closing lines (the Diamond Dog message, a senior program, the history-review line).

PASS when all of these hold:
- The count is one or two, no more and none fewer.
- ABOUT THIS PET holds no struggle.
- Each struggle is a behavior a reader can picture, without threat or clinical words ("aggressive", "resource guarding", "reactive") and without softening it until no behavior is left.
- The sentences that state the counted struggles are a small share of all the text under the four headings, roughly a fifth or less by word count. Leave the support and closing lines out of the total; keep HOUSEHOLD FIT in it.
- The profile gives the adopter no handling instruction or management plan ("let him come to you", "never reach for his bowl", "needs a home that will work on his guarding").

FAIL when any one fails, naming the struggles counted and which condition failed.
