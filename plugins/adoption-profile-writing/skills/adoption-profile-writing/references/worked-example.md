# Worked Example: Annotating a Published Card

A published card, annotated line by line: what a rewrite cuts, what it keeps, and why. The dog is invented to illustrate the rules; `[Name]` stands in for her name and `[Shelter]` for the shelter. The card runs 182 narrative words. It replaced an older card that carried four conditions on the adopter and no behavioral disclosure.

```
ABOUT THIS PET
[Name] catches a flying disc in midair. She leaps for it, trots it back, and drops it at your feet. She will chase the disc for forty minutes, and she jogs two miles on leash without pulling. She likes squeaky toys, digging in sand, puzzle feeders, and car rides.

HOUSEHOLD FIT
[Name] lived with another dog in her last home and plays well with smaller dogs in the shelter playgroup. On leash, she reacts to passing dogs. She has walked past the shelter cats without reacting, but she stalks birds in the yard. She is more comfortable with older teens and adults, and would do best in a home without young children.

TRAINING
[Name] knows sit, down, wait, leave it, and drop it, and she is learning come. She sits at every doorway without being asked and works eagerly for toys. She is one of [Shelter]'s Diamond Dogs, so [Shelter] can provide training support before and after adoption.

GOOD TO KNOW
[Name] is possessive of her toys. Vacuum cleaners frighten her. [Shelter] will go over all of her known behaviors with you before you take her home.
```

## What the copy does

**The opening is a behavior, not a category.** "[Name] catches a flying disc in midair," then three verbs describing exactly what the game looks like with her. An adopter can picture the yard.

**Every household-fit answer is present, and each names its setting.** Lived with another dog, plays in playgroup, reacts on leash. The three settings carry different behavior, and a single "good with dogs" label would have hidden that.

**The cat answer reports the observation rather than a label.** It says what she did around the cats she has passed. If a "No Cats" tag sat beside this sentence with no observation behind it, that would be a tag contradiction for staff to settle before the card ships.

**The kids limit stays; its source does not.** "More comfortable with older teens and adults" is an attribute of the dog, and the shelter's system publishes it as a search filter either way. The copy does not repeat, name, or summarize whatever report sits behind it.

**Training lists cues by name.** Five known, one in progress. That tells an adopter more about trainability than any adjective would, and it replaced an "I do best with experienced dog owners" line that nothing in the record supported.

**The closing line points at a further conversation** without publishing what that conversation will cover. Its phrasing is out of date: see the open items below.

## What a rewrite leaves out, and why

**Serious incidents do not appear**, which is the rule rather than an omission. They go to the pre-meet consultation, where a person states the circumstances and answers what the adopter asks back. What the card owes the reader is the closing that tells them the conversation is coming.

**Conditions on the adopter come out.** A fenced-yard requirement with nothing in the record behind it, and an experience requirement that trainer notes contradict, are elimination criteria: they shrink the adopter pool and say nothing about the animal.

**Affection claims the record does not support come out.** "Volunteer favorite," "big softie," snuggles, and belly rubs stay off a card when no note records them, and especially when the recorded close-contact observations are cautionary. An adopter who reaches for a belly rub is acting on the card.

**Intake-week language comes out.** "Settling into my new surroundings" describes a newly arrived animal, not one that has been at the shelter for months.

**House-training is not mentioned** when nobody knows it. That is the default for a shelter animal and needs no disclosure.

**No handling instruction survives.** A struggle on the card stops at the behavior: no management plan, no advice about which toys to put away.

## Open items on this card

This is the card as published. Six of its lines break the rules in `SKILL.md`. Do not copy any of them. The example to copy is the one in `profile-template.md`.

- **The card has room for one more struggle, not two.** Leash barking at dogs in HOUSEHOLD FIT is already one of the card's two. The record holds two more candidates: guarding her toys and her bed, and barking and backing away when a stranger walks up. Pick one, preferring the one the record gives context or progress for: "takes a little time to warm up to new people: she barks and backs away when a stranger walks up, and comes forward once they crouch." The other goes to the history review, and the closing line says so.
- **"is possessive of her toys"** names no behavior a reader can picture, and "possessive" is a personality label. If guarding is the struggle the card keeps, write the behavior and where it was seen: "likes to keep her toys to herself at the shelter, and may freeze and growl if someone reaches for one she is holding." Otherwise it goes to the history review with the rest.
- **"Vacuum cleaners frighten her"** needs a source. When nothing in the record mentions vacuums or household noise, the line comes off.
- **"reacts to passing dogs"** uses the shelter's word. Write "barks and lunges toward dogs she passes."
- **The Diamond Dog line sits in TRAINING, in retired wording.** The program support is the offset to what GOOD TO KNOW discloses, so it moves down to GOOD TO KNOW, after the behaviors it answers, as the Diamond Dog message in `profile-template.md`, word for word.
- **"before you take her home"** is the retired closer. It assumes the adoption and defers the disclosure to the last step. For a Diamond Dog, the Diamond Dog message replaces it and the support line together, and it says [Shelter] reviews her history before you meet her. For any other animal with a serious incident on file, write "[Shelter] will review [Name]'s full history with you before you meet [him/her]."
